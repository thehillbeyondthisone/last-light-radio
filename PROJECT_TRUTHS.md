## What we are making
- **Last Light Radio** is a connected fictional lo-fi world expressed through engaging, long-form animated episodes; its motto is **“Music for places that are still awake.”**
- The **software** is a separate, general-purpose desktop creator. It must work with an arbitrary creator-supplied scene/image and world, not just Last Light Radio, Seoul, pixel art, or a specific character.
- Each scene has a preserved **canonical master still** (e.g. `MASTER.png`), a locked visual identity/camera/composition, a library of approved motion clips, and scene-specific behavior rules.
- Episodes are typically **60–90 minutes, configurable by the creator**; a shorter test duration is fine for validation. The result is lived-in activity, not a four-hour repetition of a five-second loop.
- **Current creator pilot target (2026-10-24): ten interesting minutes, with meaningful events about 45–120 seconds apart.** Continuous rain and subtle idle motion are background atmosphere, not extra events counted toward that spacing. General longer-episode support remains a separate product goal.
- The user can define **several variations of each idle/action** (different page turns, tea sips, glances, cat motions, etc.). A rules-based director uses frequency, weights, cooldowns, preconditions and continuity to avoid obvious repetition.
- **No LLM or interactive AI agent makes production/story decisions at episode-assembly time.** AI generative models may produce source clips in ComfyUI; the unattended episode director selects *approved* assets according to explicit rules.
- ComfyUI runs on **another computer on the LAN**. Reconnection, availability checks, workflow compatibility and discovery where technically possible are desired, with manual URL fallback. Do not promise automatic discovery on networks where the server has not advertised itself.
- The user runs **MiniMax H3 locally via ComfyUI on an RTX 4070**. The recovered workflow and successful LAN smoke tests verify this path; preserve it. General performance and peak VRAM remain unmeasured.
- Rendering must be **model/workflow-agnostic**: H3 via the user's workflow first, with optional future Wan/LTX/etc. adapters.

## Shared world — non-negotiable
- Episodes are **linked chapters/views of one persistent universe**, not unrelated backgrounds. Shared places, routes, characters, animals, radio/music, events, time of day and changing seasons must be recognizable and consistent.
- Episodes **stand alone**, while repeat viewers can discover subtle connections through recurring details rather than explanatory exposition.
- Keep **World Bible/Registry + persistent chronology** separate from **Episode Director + scene-specific motion library**. World facts have stable IDs and versioned revisions. Draft episodes do **not** rewrite established canon; promotion/approval commits only validated changes.
- The creator can author background crossovers, cameo conditions and small events. The software schedules/uses only those allowed facts/events; it must not silently invent lore.
- **Initial world motifs recovered from early planning (candidates, not locked canon):** UMBRA FM 88.3, recurring convenience store, bus route 271, neighborhood cat, connected apartments/streets, fictional bands, recurring characters, seasonal progression.
- **“Summer Rain”** is an early episode concept. The initial apartment-above-store concept was followed by a request for a **street-level view across an intersection**; preserve both references without silently choosing one as the definitive first master shot.
- The initial fictional brand/visual direction referenced a cozy **PS1/SNES-inspired pixel aesthetic**, but the application must support any suitable source style.

## Visual behavior editor
- Let users **select a person, animal, object, or environment region in a scene** (manual hit region is enough for v1).
- Define actor states, idles, action pools, multiple clip variations, weights, timing windows, cooldowns, preconditions, transition/recovery clips, rare events and other entities' interactions.
- Support **separate logical schedules** for people, animals, ambient effects, audio and world events. Do not assume independent video clips can be safely layered; full-frame AI outputs must be scheduled exclusively until layer/mask compositing is proven.

## Media/continuity rules
- Never modify or overwrite an imported master image. Store hashes and derivatives separately.
- An approved motion clip records its input/master ID, workflow provenance, actor/action, start state, end state, duration/frame rate, visual restrictions, tags and review status.
- Only choose a clip when its **entry state** matches the current timeline state; on completion, apply its **exit state**. Idle loops preferably return to the same visual anchor. Provide explicit recovery transitions where necessary.
- Protect geometry, camera, character identity, prop placement and palette; not every generated take will pass review.
- Use static holds and independently validated ambience (rain, lighting, sound) between infrequent meaningful motions. Avoid generating 90 minutes of novel AI footage.
- Audio and video are separable tracks; honor music ownership/licensing and export metadata.
- Save the episode seed, world revision, selected clips, timecodes and rejection/fallback reasons for deterministic replay.

## Practical scope and status
- **Not yet verified here:** peak VRAM/performance benchmark, production loop quality, packaging format, target delivery settings, automatic segmentation and overlapping-mask occlusion. The limited disjoint-mask compositor is verified below.
- **First proof:** one canonical still, one character, simple idle/action variants, a deterministic 10-minute timeline and actual export. Then prove shared-world continuity with a second location/episode.
- **Do not overbuild:** no required auto-generated lore, 3D reconstruction, cloud account system, multiplayer, public hosting, model training or unattended approval of AI clips for MVP.
